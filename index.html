About

AI is rapidly becoming part of how people learn, teach, and develop expertise: students use it to explain concepts and complete assignments, educators are rethinking how they teach and assess, and professionals increasingly learn on the job alongside AI assistants and agents. This creates tremendous opportunities to expand access to expertise and tailor learning to each person, but making AI more capable is not enough on its own. A system that boosts a person’s immediate performance may not improve, and may even undermine, their long-term knowledge, skills, judgment, or independence; thoughtfully designed AI could instead enable learning that is more personalized, interactive, accessible, and integrated with real-world activity. This raises a fundamental question: as AI becomes embedded in the activities through which people learn, how should education and human learning themselves evolve?

A frontier Interdisciplinary AI & Education Conversation: The ReLearn Workshop brings together researchers and practitioners across machine learning, natural language processing, human–computer interaction, education, learning sciences, cognitive science, and related fields. We take a broad view of learning, spanning formal classrooms, higher education, professional training, workplace learning, and self-directed everyday learning.

Two guiding questions

Q1
How should education and learning adapt to AI?

How should the roles of teachers and learners change? How should curricula, assessment, and learning activities be redesigned when AI assistance is widely available? What knowledge and expertise should education continue to cultivate, and what new competencies do people need to work effectively with AI?

Q2
How should we build and evaluate AI that supports human learning?

Which forms of AI assistance improve learning, and which encourage over-reliance or deskilling? What interaction paradigms help people acquire knowledge, skills, and judgment rather than merely complete tasks? How should we model learners and evaluate the long-term effects of AI on human capability?

From “what can AI do for us?” toward “what can people learn and become with AI?”

Workshop goals

  • Shift the evaluation target from how well AI performs a task to how well people learn when AI is involved, surfacing methods, benchmarks, and evidence for AI systems that build, rather than replace, human competence.
  • Build a shared venue where three groups that rarely meet in one room exchange problems and solutions: researchers in ML/NLP and AI in education; educators redesigning teaching around AI; and professionals who learn on the job alongside AI assistants and agents.

Call for Papers

We welcome submissions from machine learning and NLP as well as HCI, AI in education, learning sciences, cognitive science, economics, and policy. Educators and learning scientists who do not usually publish at ML venues are especially encouraged to submit.

Tracks

  • Technical track: new methods, systems, empirical studies, datasets, and benchmarks.
  • Non-technical track: position papers, conceptual and policy analyses, and reports from teaching or deployment practice, including how educational systems should respond to AI.

Formats

  • Full papers ≤ 9 pages
  • Short papers ≤ 4 pages
  • Tiny papers ≤ 2 pages for late-breaking ideas, early results, or fresh perspectives

Page limits exclude references and appendices. All submissions use the ICLR 2027 style and are submitted via OpenReview. Each submission receives at least two reviews from reviewers matched to its track.

Accepted papers are non-archival. They will be public on OpenReview and this website, and authors may later submit them to archival venues. All accepted papers are presented as posters, and three will be selected for 10-minute contributed talks.

Themes & Topics

Building AI Systems for Human Learning

Redesigning the AI development pipeline (data, training, evaluation) to explicitly optimize for human learning, not just task completion or productivity.

Preserving Cognitive Engagement & Academic Integrity

Cognitive offloading and over-reliance; human–AI interaction designs that sustain critical thinking; reliable assessment where AI use is prevalent.

Everyday Learning Beyond Classrooms

AI as explicit tutor and as implicit teacher through everyday clarifications and feedback; workplace upskilling, e.g., simulated patients to train counselors.

Redefining Learning & Human–AI Collaboration

New learning objectives, AI-mediated pedagogies, personalized tutoring, human–AI collaboration, and human capabilities that complement AI.

Rethinking Assessment in the Age of AI

New approaches to assessing learning, understanding, and human capability when models can perform the tasks once used to measure them.

Equity & Access to AI-Mediated Learning

Who benefits, who may be excluded, and how AI can be designed and deployed to promote equitable access to high-quality learning.

Interdisciplinary Roundtable

The roundtable centers on the workshop’s guiding question: AI + education is at a turning point; where should we go from here? Before the workshop, we will collect challenges through a short public form from AI researchers, learning scientists, educators, and professionals (e.g., “students submit AI-generated work they cannot explain,” “I can’t tell whether my team is learning or just delegating to the agent”). Organizers will cluster these into 6–8 themes.

During the 75-minute session, participants join themed tables of 8–12 people, deliberately mixed across disciplines and roles and led by an organizer or invited speaker. Tables refine the challenge and sketch research directions, then report back to the room. A summary of each table’s challenges and proposed directions will be published here after the workshop.

Poster Session

All accepted papers are presented in a 2.5-hour poster session alongside lunch. If authors cannot attend in person, organizers will print and display their posters, and authors may link a pre-recorded video on this website.

Inclusion & Accessibility

ReLearn is committed to diverse participation across disciplines, career stages, and backgrounds. We will support participants who need it, whether through remote accessibility, travel funds (subject to sponsorship), or other help. If travel restrictions prevent attendance, we will display posters on authors’ behalf, post talk videos and slides on this website, and share roundtable outcomes publicly.

ReLearn follows the ICLR Code of Conduct and is committed to a harassment-free experience for everyone.
